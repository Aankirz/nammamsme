# PRODUCT.md

register: product

## What this is

A working surface for a small Indian business's paperwork. Documents arrive carrying a number, a date, and a consequence. This reads them, tells the owner what each one costs and when, and on a tax notice files the reply.

Not a dashboard. Not an ERP. A case file.

## Users

**Primary: the owner.** Textile trader in Ludhiana, roughly 50, four employees, turnover about ₹3 crore. Reads English but not legal English. Works on a laptop at a desk, not a phone. He is mildly anxious when he opens this, because something arrived and he does not know what it costs him.

**Secondary: his chartered accountant.** Handles 150 clients, lives in this kind of document all day, wants density and speed. Will judge the product on whether the numbers are right and traceable.

Both are in a task. Neither wants to be delighted. They want to stop worrying.

## Tone

Plain English. Short sentences. Never legal register, never chirpy.

- "₹5,12,000 demanded. Reply by 14 August."
- Not: "Tax liability of INR 5,12,000 has been assessed pursuant to Section 73."
- Not: "Uh oh! Looks like you've got a notice 😬"

State the number. State the date. State what happens if ignored. Stop.

The product's voice is a good lawyer who bills honestly: calm, specific, and willing to say "I don't know" out loud.

## The one non-negotiable behaviour

When the system cannot ground a figure in the document, it refuses to act and says why. Refusal is a designed state, not an error. It must read as competence, never as failure.

This is the product's entire trust claim. Every other decision bends to it.

## Anti-references

- **SaaS dashboard.** Stat tiles, sparklines, a "welcome back" greeting. This is not analytics; there is nothing to browse.
- **Government portal.** Dense tables, unlabelled acronyms, nested tabs, everything the same weight. That is the thing we exist because of.
- **Fintech app.** Gradients, illustration, rounded-everything, an encouraging tone about money that is being taken from you.
- **Navy and saffron.** The first reflex for Indian tax software. Also the second reflex, editorial serif on cream.

## Strategic principles

1. **Money is the hero.** Amounts are the largest thing on any screen, tabular, never wrapped.
2. **Urgency is legible before it is read.** A glance says whether something is on fire.
3. **Every figure is traceable.** Any number can be tapped to see where on the page it came from. The user should never have to trust us.
4. **Density is a courtesy.** The CA has 150 clients. Do not make them scroll for facts that fit.
5. **No screen ends in a noun.** Every view ends in something the user does.
